## Question

For each system mechanic of the two games, what is its equivalent in the other game, and what must be built from scratch in each direction?

_PT-BR: para cada mecânica de sistema dos dois jogos, qual é o equivalente no outro, e o que precisa ser implementado do zero em cada direção?_

## Owner's observations to start from

- KOF 2003's Leader super is equivalent to SVC's once-per-match super (Exceed), and is sometimes the very same move (e.g. Ryo, Iori).
- KOF 2003's switch: the outgoing fighter leaves taunting while the incoming one enters with an entry attack (usually D, heavy kick).
- SVC's cancel system is the most complex.
- Some Shared Characters differ a lot between the games (Terry, Kim).

_PT-BR — observações do dono para começar:_
- _O especial de líder do KOF 2003 equivale ao especial de uso único do SVC, e às vezes é o mesmo golpe (ex.: Ryo, Iori)._
- _A troca do KOF 2003 é o personagem que sai fazendo uma provocação enquanto o que entra vem com um golpe de entrada (geralmente D, chute forte)._
- _O sistema de cancelas do SVC é o mais complexo._
- _Há personagens compartilhados bem diferentes entre os jogos (Terry, Kim)._

## Resolution

**Owner-approved policy.** The first Imported Character uses the Base Game's activation rules for its signature move and the Base Game's cancel rules. A sequence from the Donor Game carries over when it fits those rules. The first fight is 1v1. After that test, KOF 2003 as the Base Game uses its team format and Multi-Shift for Imported Characters, and SVC Chaos as the Base Game stays 1v1. Leader Super availability in KOF 2003's AES Single mode remains to be verified. The initial 1v1 covers the behavior the Base Game exposes in that mode.

The table compares the MVS systems. In each direction it distinguishes new work on the Imported Character from system logic that would only be needed to retain a Donor Game rule. The ability to reuse native game logic depends on finding where the Base Game registers the move or fighter behavior. [Existing documentation of both engines](02-existing-documentation.md)

| Mechanic and counterpart | Rule differences and evidence | SVC → KOF 2003 | KOF 2003 → SVC |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Leader Super ↔ Exceed** | Both fill the role of a signature super. KOF ties the Leader Super to the team Leader. The owner reports that SVC limits Exceed to one use per match. Ryo and Iori are candidates for comparing the moves themselves. Exact meter costs, Exceed's activation conditions and the reset boundary still need confirmation. Keeping the donor rule would require separate eligibility and use tracking across the match. | Bind the imported Leader Super to the native Exceed rules. Add the command and move behavior where needed. Keeping the donor rule would require introducing Leader eligibility and its link to the relevant meter rules. |
| **Multi-Shift ↔ no tag counterpart in SVC's 1v1** | KOF has an active fighter and reserve team members. The owner observed an exit with a taunt and an entry with an attack, often D (heavy kick). Confirm when the attack occurs, which transitions are plain switches and how availability and cost work. SVC has one fighter per side and advances between rounds. [1v1 fight in KOF 2003](05-kof2003-1v1-fight.md) | In team play, hook the fighter into native entry, exit and return-to-active states. Supply missing per-fighter animations and collision data for those states and the entry attack. The initial 1v1 has no tag. | Use the 1v1 fighter lifecycle. Bringing Multi-Shift over would require new team and reserve state, slot selection and handoff, entry and exit behavior, KO and victory flow, and team HUD support. |
| **Attack cancels ↔ attack cancels** | Compare one transition at a time, such as normal → special or special → super. For each one, identify whether it works on hit, block or whiff, the input window and any meter cost. The owner's observation that SVC's cancels are more complex is a lead. The research cites a community report of different input handling for normals (four frames in SVC and one in KOF 2003 for the reported input rule). The window for each cancel still needs confirmation. [Existing documentation of both engines](02-existing-documentation.md) | Bind imported moves to KOF's allowed transitions. Implement compatible move-specific follow-ups. Retaining a sequence that needs a KOF-only permission would require a new transition with its own timing and eligibility. |
| **Defensive cancels and movement** | The comparison is by action and situation. A move performed out of block, a way to move past an attack and a free movement action can serve different purposes. The local sources leave the exact counterpart, input, invulnerability and cost for each action open. | Connect the imported fighter to the Base Game's universal actions and supply required fighter-specific states. An unmatched donor action would require its own input, movement or block transition and collision behavior. | Connect the imported fighter to SVC's universal actions and supply required fighter-specific states. An unmatched donor action would require its own input, movement or block transition and collision behavior. |
| **Super meter ↔ super meter** | The training scripts expose life and meter for the SVC fighter and per-member life and team meter in KOF. Those fields establish the scope of the observed state. Stock caps, refill and spending rules, temporary meter modes and their effects on cancels remain open. [Existing documentation of both engines](02-existing-documentation.md) | Charge and spend meter through the KOF team rules. Translate each imported move's use of the resource. Reproducing a distinct SVC meter mode would need its own state, activation, drain and spending rules. | Charge and spend meter through SVC's fighter rules. Translate each imported move's use of the resource. Reproducing KOF team meter behavior would require shared resource state and handling changes to the active fighter and team composition. |
| **Fight progression and KO** | SVC uses 1v1 rounds. In KOF MVS team play, the active slot and each team member's life are tracked. The available research confirms an AES Single menu option and leaves the fight and native runtime support to be verified. [1v1 fight in KOF 2003](05-kof2003-1v1-fight.md) | Use the KOF 1v1 path once verified and the native team flow afterwards. A custom 1v1 would need its own setup, KO and win rules if the available path proves insufficient. | Use SVC's rounds and reset flow. Reproducing KOF team elimination would require new progression across the surviving members. |

Use the Base Game's version of each Shared Character. Compare Ryo and Iori move by move before treating any signature move as the same, including its command, hitboxes and follow-ups. Compare Terry and Kim per move as well, since their versions differ. The match rules for juggle, priority and damage follow the Base Game as set by the map. For the spec, confirm the signature move's activation conditions and cost, including whether Exceed becomes available again at the next round or only at the next match, exercise an allowed cancel and a donor-only sequence under the Base Game's rules, and check the KOF entry and exit transitions in team play if KOF is chosen. **PT-BR**

**Decisões aprovadas pelo dono.** O primeiro personagem importado usa as regras do jogo base para ativar o golpe de assinatura e fazer cancelamentos. Uma sequência do jogo doador entra quando cabe nessas regras. A primeira luta é 1x1. Depois desse teste, o KOF 2003 como jogo base usa seu formato de equipe e o Multi-Shift para os importados, e o SVC Chaos como jogo base continua 1x1. A disponibilidade do Leader Super no modo Single do AES do KOF 2003 ainda precisa ser verificada. O 1x1 inicial cobre o comportamento que o jogo base oferece nesse modo.

A tabela compara os sistemas MVS. Em cada direção, ela indica o trabalho novo no personagem importado e a lógica de sistema que só seria necessária para manter uma regra do jogo doador.
